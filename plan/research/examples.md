## 2. What each tier buys (perks are what sets the price)

| Perk | Cheapest tier that gets it | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Logo / social media mention | lowest | all four events |
| Swag handout | lowest | birmingHack, R. U. Hacking |
| Resume access | lowest (HackBelfast: early access for the top tier) | HackBelfast |
| Host a challenge | middle | birmingHack Silver, R. U. Hacking (all tiers) |
| Workshop / talk, stand, catering for attendees | middle | birmingHack Silver, HackBelfast Gold |
| Opening speech | middle (2 min) to top (5-8 min) | birmingHack, R. U. Hacking |
| Judging panel seat | top (birmingHack Gold) or silver and up (R. U. Hacking) | both |
| Meeting with winning team, problem statement contribution | top only | HackBelfast Diamond |

Pattern: prices rise with scarce access (judging, speaking time, resumes first) rather than with promised results. None of the three prospectuses promises a measurable outcome such as hires or sign-ups.

## 3. Why sponsors pay (reasons the organisers give)

- **Recruitment:** "access top talent" (HackBelfast), "find your future employees" (birmingHack), graduate recruitment as the first reason (R. U. Hacking; says hackathons are replacing career fairs).
- **Product exposure and feedback:** introduce tools and APIs for students to build with (HackBelfast, R. U. Hacking prize "branded by your company to get students to develop on your API").
- **Brand:** visibility to students across slides, signage, social media, and ongoing mentions after the event.
- **Local ecosystem:** support the regional student tech community (HackBelfast).
- **MLH's view (US-led hackathon league, general guide):** three main goals are product feedback (target developer relations staff), recruiting (target hiring and talent acquisition), and brand awareness (target marketing and PR). Sponsors worry about attendee fit, who else sponsors, package value and how professional the organiser looks. Sponsor types: cash, presenting (co-brand), in-kind (credits, hardware), media partners, community partners. Source: [MLH guide](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/understanding-your-sponsors). The guide gives no prices.

## 5. What this suggests for TBC (to test, not facts)

1. Sponsors buy access (judges, challenges, resumes), so an event profile should list the scarce access it offers.
2. Prospectuses are static PDFs sent by email or LinkedIn; the committee contact details are in the file and "negotiable" is standard. This is the manual process TBC would replace.
3. Nobody publishes outcome data. If sponsors want it, TBC can offer it; unknown until interviews.
4. Company-hosted events (Wise, Allianz, Lloyds, Pfizer) are a different buyer from student-society events: they host and recruit rather than sponsor.
