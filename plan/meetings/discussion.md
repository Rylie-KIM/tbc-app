We love our tech communities. As nerds, we do need to socialise. We all get together, connect, build fun stuff, while at the same time taking side quests to find sponsors for the next event, look for people to recruit, look for a venue and find new users for your product. Things could be a little more centralised. 

At TBC we want to bring the interested parties togethers. Outreach, hiring candidates, users, beta testers opportunities can be traded for event sponsor. Like Indeed, but for your events.

### Facts from Product lead's sponsorship experience (their own hackathon in Bristol)

- **Three kinds of sponsor:**
  1. Recruitment: big companies with mass recruitment (Accenture, Lloyds as examples) and small startups that want to hire.
  2. Product testing: younger companies with untested tools that want hundreds of people to use and break them and find use cases.
  3. Marketing and brand: for example ElevenLabs; people use the tool at the event and recommend it at their later jobs.
- **Sponsorship tiers** (amounts in pounds, per the team). The higher the tier, the fewer slots, because higher tiers pay for visibility and too many sponsors would dilute it. The slot limits are this hackathon's own policy, not an industry rule.

  | Tier | Price | What the sponsor gets | Slots at this hackathon |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Bronze | £500-1,000 | Logo only | Unlimited |
  | Silver | £1,000-2,000 | Logo and a bit more (what exactly: not said, to ask) | Unlimited |
  | Gold | £2,000-3,000 | Sets a challenge for participants | Maximum 6 |
  | Platinum | £5,000-7,500 | Event co-branded with the sponsor | Maximum 1 (probably none) |

  A typical sponsor pays about £2,000, which is the lower end for a new hackathon.
- **How sponsors are found today:** going to events, LinkedIn messages, finding a way in; every sponsor's route is different, "no one size fits all", and it is often luck. Example: a LinkedIn post led a contact who wore a PostHog shirt to introduce PostHog, which then sponsored. A reply from another company only said "we'll pass it on".
- **Current state of that hackathon:** sponsors are fine; the biggest problem is the venue (the Bristol venue pulled out, Bristol Science Park is being considered). Venue is another pain point that a platform like TBC could cover.
- **How to reach sponsors for research:** look at the sponsors of other hackathons, find them on LinkedIn and message them, or go in person; best of all, try to schedule a call on the spot. Product lead's framing for a cold message: "can I speak to you about your experience of finding hackathons to sponsor, because we want to make that process easier"; it is only a call, no money involved.

### Questions to put into the sponsor questionnaire (from the meeting)

- Why did you choose to sponsor this event in the past?
- How did you find it, and what would make finding opportunities easier?
- What do you want from the organiser side?
- Which kind are you (recruitment, product testing, marketing), and what did you get out of the last event?
- Added by me, not said in the meeting: how much do you usually pay and for which tier; what stopped you from sponsoring other events.
